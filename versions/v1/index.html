Life Journey

Biography

PA. Wesley Olajide Ojo Aiyegboka was born on the 4th of March 1943 in Bukuru, a sleepy town 5km south of Jos, Plateau State, to the late Michael Ojo Ihinmodu and Seliatu Daniyan.

He had an uncertain start in life as he lost his mother, a few months after his birth. As a boy, he was raised by his father and paternal grandmother until he lost them at the age of 17 years old. It is fair to say that Olajide faced considerable challenges from the very beginning of his life, but those early experiences taught him resilience, survival and, above all, how to face adversity head-on.

He started his primary education at St Paul’s Anglican Primary School, Iyara, in 1953 where he formed friendships that would last a lifetime. Along with some of these friends, he studied hard enough to pass the entrance examination to Provincial Secondary School, Okene, in 1959.

In addition to pursuing his academic qualifications, he was a keen hockey player at Provincial Secondary School Okene. His love for the game of Hockey led him to playing against schools from different provinces; an experience he cherished and was proud of. In 1963, he finished from Provincial Secondary School in Okene and proceeded to Barewa College Zaria for his Higher School Certificate (HSC).

“He knew the true meaning of giving and gave to everyone he thought needed assistance.”

At Barewa college, he completed his Higher School Certificate in 1965 with good grades and proceeded to study for a degree in Economics at Ahmadu Bello University, Zaria, doing life the only way he knows best – very large!

Upon completion of a Bachelor of Science in Economics in 1969, Olajide began his career with the Ministry of Economic Development in Ilorin, Kwara State. On 25th of April 1972, he married his Wife, Helen Abidemi Ojo Aiyegboka of blessed memory.

In his pursuit of further education, Olajide travelled to the United States of America for postgraduate study, where he earned a Masters degree in International Economics at Vanderbilt University, Tennessee, graduating in 1976 as a member of the 1975/1976 cohort. Thereafter, he returned to Nigeria and resumed work with the Ministry of Economic Planning in Ilorin, Kwara State. In 1981, he joined the Nigerian Bank for Commerce and Industry (NBCI), now the Bank of Industry, in Bauchi State and was subsequently transferred to the bank’s head office in Lagos in 1985, where he rose through the ranks to become an Assistant General Manager. He left the Bank in 1991, went into independent contracting and, for some time, managed a frozen food enterprise in Ilorin alongside his wife. Throughout his working life and beyond, his greatest investment was not in business but in people.

He took pride in helping others build better lives for themselves and their families. He enabled payment of school fees, helped young people secure admission into higher institutions and afforded accommodation for friends and families who needed a place to stay while pursuing their education. People benefited from his generosity, done without fanfare. He simply saw opportunities to help and did what he could to support. He was unwavering in his commitment to honesty, regardless of the situation. Fully aware that his candour could sometimes rub people the wrong way, he would rather face the consequences of being truthful than compromise his principles and thought process.

“Olajide was a larger-than-life character.”

Olajide was a larger-than-life character. He was an orphan who navigated life without the benefit of parental guidance yet, managed to overcome the challenges before him and make a positive and lasting impact in the lives of many people. He knew the true meaning of giving and gave to everyone he thought needed assistance.

He came to love the Lord and became a devoted Christian. His love for Iyara was second to none; Iyara was home in a way that no other place could be, and he always expressed the wish to spend his final days there. True to form, he had his way - as he so often did in life. He returned home, and when God called, he answered.

He lived a full and remarkable life, overcame adversity, built enduring friendships, raised a family, pursued education, worked hard, fearless with his convictions, rallied around others and left an indelible mark in the lives of many.

He will be deeply missed, fondly remembered and forever loved by his people.
